Recognize Phishing. Protect Yourself.

Learn how cybercriminals trick people into revealing passwords, OTPs, banking details, and personal information.


1. What Is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyberattack in which an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organization to trick victims into revealing confidential information, opening harmful attachments, or visiting fraudulent websites.


📧 Email Phishing

Fake emails pretending to come from banks, companies, colleges, or online services.

📱 Smishing

Fraudulent SMS or text messages containing suspicious links or payment requests.

☎ Vishing

Phone calls used to steal OTPs, passwords, or banking information.

🎯 Spear Phishing

Targeted phishing messages customized for a specific person or organization.

2. How to Recognize Phishing

Suspicious Sender

Check the complete email address and domain. A display name alone does not prove identity.

Urgent Language

Messages such as "Your account will be blocked immediately" pressure you to act without thinking.

Unexpected Links

Look carefully at the actual domain before opening a link. Similar-looking names may be fraudulent.

Unexpected Attachments

Unknown files may contain malware or lead to credential theft.

Requests for Secrets

Never share your password, PIN, or OTP with someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

Unusual Offers

Prize claims, refunds, and unbelievable discounts may be designed to steal information.


How to Check a Website

  • Read the domain name carefully, including its spelling.
  • HTTPS encrypts a connection but does not guarantee that a website is legitimate.
  • Do not trust a website just because it has a logo or professional design.
  • Visit the official website using a trusted bookmark or manually entered address.
  • Never enter credentials after following an unexpected login link.

3. Social Engineering Techniques

Fear

"Your bank account will be suspended."

Defense: Contact the bank through its official channel.

Urgency

"Pay within five minutes to avoid a penalty."

Defense: Pause and verify the request.

Authority

"I am from IT support. Tell me your password."

Defense: Verify identity and follow official security procedures.

Curiosity and Rewards

"You have won a free smartphone!"

Defense: Verify the offer independently.

4. Realistic Phishing Examples

The following are educational simulations, not genuine messages from the named organizations.

Example A: Fake Bank Alert

From: Security Team <alert@example.invalid>

Subject: Immediate Account Verification

Your account may be suspended. Click the link below to verify your details immediately.

Warning signs: Urgency, an unverified sender, and a request to verify sensitive information through a link.

Example B: Fake Delivery SMS

Message: Your parcel is on hold. Pay a small additional fee using the link in this message.

Warning signs: Unexpected payment request and an unverified delivery link.

Example C: Fake College Login

Message: Your student account requires immediate password verification to prevent deactivation.

Warning signs: Threat of account deactivation and an unexpected request for credentials.

What Should You Do?

  1. Do not click suspicious links or open unexpected files.
  2. Verify the message using a known official contact.
  3. Report it to your organization's IT or security team.
  4. Delete or quarantine the message according to your organization's reporting procedure.

5. Best Practices to Stay Safe

Use MFA

Enable multifactor authentication, preferably phishing-resistant passkeys or security keys when available.

Use Strong Passwords

Use unique passwords for different accounts and store them in a trusted password manager.

Update Software

Keep your operating system, browser, and security software up to date.

Verify Requests

Confirm unusual payment, password, or information requests through an independent channel.

Report Attacks

Use your email provider's phishing-report feature or your organization's security reporting process.

Act Quickly if Compromised

Change affected passwords through the official website, revoke active sessions, and contact your bank if financial information is exposed.

7. Conclusion

Phishing attacks exploit human trust rather than only technical weaknesses. By checking senders and website addresses, refusing unexpected requests for confidential information, enabling multifactor authentication, and reporting suspicious messages, users can significantly reduce their risk.
